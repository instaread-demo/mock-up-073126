Governor Pushes for Extended Bar and Restaurant Hours and Alcohol Sale at Off-Site Events for World Cup

Julia DelPozzo, Reporter • June 17, 2026
Photo courtesy of the Governor’s Office.

Gov. Kathy Hochul presented a plan to help communities and businesses participate in World Cup celebrations while ensuring it is done in a safe manner. The proposal would allow event organizers to host off-site events throughout the duration of the World Cup by applying for a World Cup One-Day Permit through the New York State Liquor Authority.

The NY/NJ area will host eight matches during the tournament. The World Cup runs from June 11 to July 19, bringing millions of fans and visitors to the United States, including New York and New Jersey.

The One-Day Permit would allow businesses and organizers to offer alcohol at off-site events. The permit would also allow pubs and restaurants to provide service in nearby outdoor locations for the duration of the tournament rather than the existing four-day limit.

The One-Day Alcohol Event Permit costs $36 per day, per point of sale. Applications must be submitted at least 15 days before an event. Applicants may request up to 12 one-day permits within a single application, and organizers planning more than 12 events may submit multiple applications.

Hochul is also working with the Legislature to temporarily authorize extended operating hours during the World Cup. The proposal would allow bars, restaurants and other establishments to remain open whenever matches are broadcast live.

The proposal comes as businesses and lawmakers look for ways to maximize the economic benefits associated with the tournament, which is projected to bring more than 1 million visitors to the region and generate billions of dollars in economic activity.

“The World Cup has been played in the United States only a single time — back in 1994 — making this summer’s event truly once in a generation,” State Sen. James Skoufis said. “We have an opportunity and responsibility to ensure New York is primed to benefit from the 1.2 million expected visitors.”

According to hospitality associations, bars and restaurants are expected to be major gathering places for fans who are unable to attend matches in person. Supporters of the proposal say it could provide additional opportunities for small businesses to benefit from increased tourism and visitor spending during the tournament.

Several logistical challenges remain, including managing large outdoor gatherings while maintaining public safety. Organizers of off-site events would still be required to obtain local approval and develop operations plans that clearly identify service areas and event procedures.

In addition to the permit proposal, state officials have announced transportation and community initiatives tied to the World Cup. According to state officials, shuttle capacity will increase significantly during the tournament, with certain matches offering as many as 18,000 shuttle seats.

To make transportation more accessible, Hochul and the FIFA World Cup 2026 NYNJ Host Committee announced a reduction in official stadium shuttle costs, lowering round-trip fares from $80 to $20. The plan also reserves 20 percent of shuttle tickets for New York residents.

“Hosting the World Cup is a once-in-a-generation opportunity for New Yorkers, and we are making this world-class event as affordable and accessible as possible,” Hochul said in a statement.

The statewide World Cup effort also includes free public viewing events and community celebrations. Hochul, Mayor Zohran Mamdani and the FIFA World Cup 2026 NYNJ Host Committee announced free fan events in all five boroughs of New York City, along with large-scale viewing events on Long Island and in the Mid-Hudson region.

State officials have also announced funding for 12 community projects through the New York State Community World Cup Grant Program. Recipients include organizations and municipalities in the Capital Region, Central New York, the Finger Lakes, Southern Tier, Mohawk Valley, North Country and Western New York.

The governor has also proposed the NY Kicks initiative, which would expand youth soccer opportunities through investments in facilities and programming in underserved communities. The initiative is part of a broader effort to connect communities across the state to the World Cup experience and its anticipated economic and cultural impact.
