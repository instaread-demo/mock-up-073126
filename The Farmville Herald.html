Severe storm causes widespread damage, power outages

Published 4:50 pm Monday, July 27, 2026

By Jeff Moore

A severe storm that moved through Farmville late Sunday night left a widespread path of damage across the town and surrounding areas, downing trees and power lines, damaging homes and buildings and and knocking out electrical service.

The National Weather Service office in Wakefield said the storm strengthened as it moved over Farmville, producing what preliminary assessments indicated were straight-line winds of at least 70 mph.

Nancy Moll, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service office in Wakefield, said the storm developed in an environment that was highly favorable for severe weather.

“It was very good environment for severe weather last night,” Moll said.

She said the storm “pulsed up” as it moved over Farmville, meaning it strengthened rapidly in the area.

“When I was talking to one of the forecasters this morning, what he said was it looks like based on damage, it was likely 70 miles per hour or greater for the wind damage,” Moll said.

She said the storm produced widespread damage, noting they received a lot of reports from the area. 

“There wasn’t really any sign or signature of a tornado,” Moll said. “It’s definitely more likely that it’s straight line (wind) damage.”

Farmville Fire Department Chief Daniel Clark said the department began receiving storm-related calls late Sunday night and continued responding to calls Monday.

“Our first call was around 11:30 last night when we started getting calls for storm related incidents,” Clark said.

The storm brought down multiple trees and power lines, resulting in road closures in several areas, Clark said. The damage was widespread across the northern portion of Farmville, extending from the avenues area to Industrial Park Road, Cedar Avenue and Early Street.

“The damage was not localized into one area,” he said. “It’s kind of spread across the north end of town.” 

Farmville Town Manager Scott Davis, LP.D., said town crews and emergency personnel worked through the night and into Monday responding to the storm’s aftermath.

Davis said several trees had fallen on power lines, including one on High Street and another on Cedar Lane. Dominion Energy had been notified, and utility crews were working to remove trees from power lines and restore service.

Clark said Dominion had 16 contract crews assisting local crews with power restoration efforts.

Dominion Energy spokesman Craig Carper said the storm that passed through Farmville caused notable damage in the area.

“This included downed wires, poles and crossarms. Roughly 5,000 customers were affected in total,” Carper said. “We’ve made significant progress on restoration since then. There were a total of 87 work sites for restoration. That number is now down to 24.”

He said Dominion Energy dispatched all available crews in the area and we should have everyone back up by Monday night.

As of 9 a.m. Monday, Dominion Energy’s outage map shows more than 1,500 customers in and around Farmville without power.

The town’s water treatment and wastewater treatment plants were operating on generators Monday, along with two sewer pump stations, Davis said.

The storm also produced hail in the Farmville area. 

Moll said the National Weather Service received reports of quarter-size hail south of Farmville, while another report indicated hail covered the ground near Longwood University.

Moll said rainfall totals in the Farmville area generally ranged from about a half-inch to 1 inch, with some reports indicating slightly more.

The storm caused significant damage to private property, including two homes where trees fell into residential structures, Clark said.

Residents of the two homes were relocated with assistance from the American Red Cross, he said.

“There was significant damage to their house, as well as some other trees that did some moderate damage to other homes,” Clark said.

Other damage reported by the fire department included a camper that overturned off Industrial Park Road. All of the occupants of the camper were reported to be safe, although Clark said one person from an adjacent camper appeared to have suffered a minor injury and was treated at the scene by Prince Edward Rescue.

Clark also reported damage to the Green Front Furniture building on First Street and the Presbyterian Church on West Third Street.

Moll said the National Weather Service also received a report from Prince Edward County of a church whose roof had been peeled back by the storm’s winds.

No other storm-related injuries were known to Clark.

The storm’s damage was not concentrated in a single location, with emergency crews continuing to respond Monday to reports of downed trees and wires.

Clark described the storm as “very significant” and “one of the worst we’ve had in quite a while” in Farmville.

Moll said the storm was part of a pattern that has been conducive to severe weather, noting that similar storms have produced significant damage in other areas.

“It was just a very good environment and we’re kind of in a pattern,” Moll said.

The storm did not produce flooding in the Farmville area, Moll said.

While the National Weather Service’s preliminary assessment pointed to straight-line winds rather than a tornado, the agency continued to review reports and damage information.

Davis said the full extent of damage to private property was not yet known Monday.

“As to the extent of damage to private property, I cannot give you an accurate account at this time,” Davis said.

Town crews and emergency personnel remained active Monday as utility crews worked to clear trees from power lines and restore electricity.

Dominion Energy has not yet responded to a request for additional information on the power outages and damage to the local electrical infrastructure.
