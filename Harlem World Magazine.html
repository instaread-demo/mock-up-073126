Expand Pathways To Affordable Home Ownership From Harlem And Beyond

July 27, 2026

Homeownership has long been one of the few reliable ways for New Yorkers to build both housing stability and wealth.

But as home prices have skyrocketed while incomes have failed to keep pace, the prospect of ownership has slipped out of reach for more and more low- and moderate-income households.

“The number of Black homeowner households (show)… a 14.7 percent decline…”

The consequences are visible in the city’s widening homeownership gaps. The number of Black homeowner households in the city fell from 209,524 in 2002 to 178,659 in 2023, a 14.7 percent decline, even as the total number of owner-occupied units increased 13 percent during the same period. 

The city has several promising programs that could help reverse this trend and boost pathways to ownership, but most are small-scale. The HomeFirst program provides down-payment assistance to lower-income first-time homebuyers, but it has helped just over 3,000 households secure homeownership in nearly two decades. Open Door, which helps finance the construction of new affordable homes for first-time buyers, has financed only 451 units across 10 projects since its launch in 2017. The Affordable Neighborhood Cooperative Program, which helps renovate distressed city-owned rental buildings and convert them into affordable co-ops for existing residents, has created roughly 700 units of homeownership since 2014. And HomeFix, which provides low- or no-interest and forgivable loans for repairs to one- to four-family homes, had assisted just 94 homeowners from its launch in 2019 through June 2022. 

City leaders have begun to take important steps to advance homeownership goals. The recently adopted city budget includes more than $5 million for homeowner stabilization services.

“…preserve their homes and retain wealth…”

But more should be done. In our report, 5 Ideas to Advance Economic Mobility in NYC, we urge the Mamdani administration and City Council to launch a major new affordable homeownership initiative with two clear goals: helping 5,000 lower- and moderate-income households become first-time homeowners and helping 5,000 current lower-income homeowners preserve their homes and retain wealth by the end of 2028, writes The Center for an Urban Future.

5 Ideas to Advance Economic Mobility in NYC

Check out all five of the policy proposals in our new report outlining how city leaders can tackle the affordability crisis through policies that boost incomes, increase economic mobility, and expand pathways to the middle class.

The ideas include

  • Help transform CUNY into the nation’s leading public university system for adult learners.
  • Tap tech-savvy CUNY students to help small businesses adopt AI.
    Launch an AI Workforce Preparation Fund and Citywide AI Workforce Task Force.
  • Expand pathways to affordable homeownership so more lower-income New Yorkers can build stability and wealth.
  • Strengthen CityFHEPS navigation to help more New Yorkers secure stable housing and build economic mobility.

To read more about each idea, check out the report, 5 Ideas to Advance Economic Mobility in NYC.
